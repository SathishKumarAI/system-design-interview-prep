# Behavioural question bank

Forty questions, eight themes, six stories. The grouping is the point: prepare a
**theme**, not a question, or you will produce forty thin answers and be unable
to go one level deeper on any of them.

Each theme names what the interviewer is actually testing. They are not asking
what happened; they are asking whether you can be trusted with a decision.

## 1. Conflict and disagreement

*Testing: can you hold a position without making it personal, and change it
without making it a defeat.*

| Question | What the strong version has |
|---|---|
| Tell me about a disagreement with a colleague | The disagreement was technical, you can still state THEIR case fairly, and the resolution was evidence and not seniority |
| A time you disagreed with your manager | You escalated once, in writing, then committed — "disagree and commit" only counts if you can name what you committed to |
| Someone on your team was not pulling their weight | You went to them before you went to anyone else |
| You received feedback you disagreed with | You acted on the half that was true |

## 2. Influence without authority

*Testing: staff-level work is mostly this.*

| Question | What the strong version has |
|---|---|
| You convinced a team to change direction | A document, a number, and the objection you had to answer |
| Drove a decision across teams that did not report to you | Who you talked to FIRST, and why that order mattered |
| Got buy-in for something unglamorous — a migration, a paydown | You made someone else's problem go away as the price of it |

## 3. Failure and mistakes

*Testing: whether you know what you did wrong, in specific terms.*

| Question | What the strong version has |
|---|---|
| Tell me about a time you failed | You caused it. Not "the vendor", not "the deadline" |
| A production incident you were responsible for | The timeline, the blast radius, and what you changed so the class of thing cannot recur |
| A decision you would make differently now | It was defensible THEN with what you knew, and you can say what new information changed it |
| A project that did not ship | You can name when you should have killed it, and why you did not |

## 4. Ambiguity and ownership

| Question | What the strong version has |
|---|---|
| You had to act without enough information | The reversible/irreversible call: you moved fast where it was cheap to undo |
| You saw a problem nobody owned | You either fixed it or found it an owner. "I raised it" is not ownership |
| Priorities changed mid-flight | What you stopped doing. There is always something |

## 5. Scale, depth and technical judgement

*The bridge to the design rounds — these should reuse your actual systems.*

| Question | What the strong version has |
|---|---|
| The most technically difficult thing you have built | Difficulty stated as a CONSTRAINT, not a word count |
| A time you chose the boring technology | The exciting option, and what it would have cost |
| You had to make something faster | The measurement BEFORE, the bottleneck you found, the number after |
| Introduced something that later became a problem | The trade-off was known and you priced it wrong |

## 6. Leading and growing others

| Question | What the strong version has |
|---|---|
| Mentored someone | What THEY can do now that they could not |
| Had to give hard feedback | It was specific, early, and in private |
| Disagreed with a hiring decision | |

## 7. Customer and business sense

| Question | What the strong version has |
|---|---|
| Said no to a customer or a stakeholder | You offered the thing behind the request |
| Traded engineering quality against a date | You named the debt and it got paid |
| Something you built that nobody used | You found out why |

## 8. The closers

Asked last, worth rehearsing precisely because they feel like small talk.

- Why are you leaving? *(Never about people. About what you want to do next.)*
- Why this company? *(One specific thing, not their marketing.)*
- What do you want to be doing in three years?
- What questions do you have for me? *(Have three. One about the work, one
  about the team, one about what would make them regret hiring you.)*

## Company flavours

See [../09-company-styles/](../09-company-styles/README.md) for how each runs
the loop. The short version:

| Company | What their behavioural round is really for |
|---|---|
| Amazon | The leadership principles, literally. Two per interviewer, scored |
| Google | Googleyness — collaboration and ambiguity, rarely conflict |
| Meta | Speed and impact; expect "what was YOUR contribution" three times |
| Startups | Whether you can do the job with nobody to ask |

## How to practise this

Pick a question, set two minutes, answer out loud, score it against
[rubric.md](rubric.md). Two minutes is the target: past three you have stopped
answering and started narrating.

The app serves these one at a time and feeds the same review queue as everything
else, so a weak answer comes back and a strong one recedes.
