# Story inventory

**Status is `seed` on purpose, and only you can move it.** Everything else in
this repo could be written by someone who knows the subject. This cannot: the
entire value of a behavioural answer is that it happened to you, with a number
attached and a consequence you can name.

Filling six of these is about two hours. It is the highest-return preparation in
the whole loop, because these six are reused in every interview you sit for the
rest of your career — and because six stories cover
[forty questions](question-bank.md) once they are chosen for coverage rather
than for fondness.

## How to choose six

Not your six proudest. Your six most **load-bearing** — the ones that carry the
most themes each.

1. **The hardest technical thing.** Difficulty as a constraint, not an adjective.
2. **The one that failed.** Yours, caused by you. Do not skip this slot; it is
   the one interviewers probe hardest and the one nobody prepares.
3. **The conflict.** A disagreement you can still state the other side of fairly.
4. **The one you drove across teams.** Influence with no authority.
5. **The judgement call.** Two defensible options, you picked, you can price the
   one you rejected.
6. **The one where you changed your mind.** New evidence, publicly reversed.

Then check coverage: map each to the eight themes in the bank. **A theme with no
story is the question that will sink you**, and it is far cheaper to find that
out here than in the room.

## Template — copy this block six times

```markdown
### Story N — <a name only you use, e.g. "the reindex">

**One line:** <what happened, in a sentence a stranger would understand>

**Themes it covers:** conflict · influence · failure · ambiguity · depth · leading · customer

**Situation** — when, where, what was at stake.
_2 sentences. The listener must know why it was hard before you say what you did._

**Task** — what was specifically yours.
_1 sentence. If you cannot say "I was the one who…", this is someone else's story._

**Action** — what you did, and the decision inside it.
_4-6 sentences. Include the option you rejected and what it would have cost.
This is the part that separates a senior answer from a staff one._

**Result** — with a NUMBER.
_Users, latency, money, weeks, headcount. If you genuinely cannot get a number,
say what you can observe instead — and know this story scores lower for it._

**What I changed afterwards** — concretely enough that someone could check.
_"I learned a lot" scores 1. "I now write the rollback before the rollout" scores 3._

**Where it goes deeper** — the follow-up you expect, and your answer.
_They will ask one level down. Decide now which level you want that to be._
```

## Rules that keep these usable

| Rule | Why |
|---|---|
| **Write them down, do not memorise them** | A memorised answer is audible, and it cannot be re-cut for a different question |
| **One story may serve three themes** — say so in the header | That is the entire economy of this file |
| **Keep the number even when it is unflattering** | "Cut p99 from 4s to 900ms" is a story. "Made it much faster" is a claim |
| **Update after every real interview** | The follow-up they asked that you had not prepared is the most valuable line you will ever add here |
| **Never invent one** | It collapses on the second follow-up, and it collapses in a way that ends the loop |
