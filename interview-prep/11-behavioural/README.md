# Behavioural — the half that fails people who can design

A staff-level loop is usually four technical rounds and one behavioural, and the
behavioural one is the round candidates prepare least and lose most often. Not
because the questions are hard — they are famously predictable — but because the
answers are *specific to you*, and specificity cannot be revised the night before.

## Where to look

| Question | File |
|---|---|
| What will they actually ask, and which theme is it probing? | [question-bank.md](question-bank.md) |

## The one idea

**You do not prepare answers. You prepare stories.**

Forty questions map onto about eight themes, and eight themes map onto about six
stories from your own career. A story that carries conflict, a decision you owned,
a number, and something you would do differently answers "tell me about a
disagreement", "a time you influenced without authority" and "a mistake you made"
without being three different preparations.

The failure mode is the opposite: preparing per question, producing forty thin
answers, and being unable to go one level deeper on any of them when asked.

## What this folder cannot do for you

The question bank and the rubric are generic and they are written. **The stories
are not, and nobody else can write them** — the entire value of a behavioural
answer is that it happened to you, with a number attached and a consequence you
can name.

`story-inventory.md` is a template with the slots empty on purpose. Filling six of
them is roughly two hours of real work and it is the single highest-return
preparation in the whole loop, because those six stories are reused in every
interview you sit for the rest of your career.

## How this is practised

The app serves one question at a time, times it, and scores it against the rubric
in this folder — the same shape as a system-design mock, feeding the same review
queue. A weak answer comes back; a strong one recedes.

Two minutes is the target. A behavioural answer that runs past three minutes has
stopped being an answer and started being a story about a story.
