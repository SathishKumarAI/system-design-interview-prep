# Newsletters and Substacks

Subscribe to **three at most**. A full inbox is a to-do list you never finish; three is a habit.

## System design

| Newsletter | Author | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| [ByteByteGo](https://blog.bytebytego.com/) | Alex Xu | **The default.** One complex system per issue, broken into a clear diagram. Huge readership for good reason. Free tier is enough |
| [The System Design Newsletter](https://newsletter.systemdesign.one/) | Neo Kim | In-depth case studies of real architectures. Excellent for "how did a real company do this" |
| [System Design Classroom](https://newsletter.systemdesignclassroom.com/) | Raul Junco | Practical, interview-shaped, good on fundamentals |
| [Quastor](https://quastor.org/) | — | Summarises big-tech engineering blog posts. **Best value per minute** if you only read one |
| [System Design but Simple](https://www.systemdesignbutsimple.com/) | Stephane Moreau | Approachable; has a good free-resources roundup |
| [AlgoMaster](https://blog.algomaster.io/) | Ashish Pratap Singh (ex-Amazon) | Same author as `awesome-system-design-resources`; coding + design |

## How to read them without drowning

1. **Three subscriptions.** For you: ByteByteGo (breadth), Quastor (blog summaries), and one of
   Chip Huyen / Eugene Yan (your ML track).
2. **One reading slot a week**, 30 minutes. Not daily, not in the inbox.
3. **Turn one issue a month into a drill** — read the requirements, close the tab, design it,
   then diff. That single habit is worth more than the other 51 issues.
4. **Archive aggressively.** Unread newsletters generate guilt, not skill.
